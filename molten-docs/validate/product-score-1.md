## Red flags

- 🔴 **Solution-first pitch.** The pitch starts from "we can use AI to help with this," and interest was collected *after* showing the product. You're validating that people like your idea, not that they have the problem badly.
- 🔴 **Free tools already cover most of the job.** A free AI chat tool for drafting plus a free scheduler tier (or the platforms' own scheduling) handles roughly 80% of this workflow at no cost. You need a reason someone would pay *and* switch.
- 🔴 **Vague target customer.** "Entrepreneurs and small companies" covers millions of people with different jobs. You can't write a message, pick a channel, or set a price for all of them.
- 🔴 **Interest, not intent to pay.** Waitlist sign-ups and "want to try it" cost the person nothing. Nobody has pre-paid.
- 🔴 **Well-trodden idea that founders keep building because they feel the pain.** AI cross-posting and repurposing tools are one of the most common ideas built since cheap AI writing arrived. Many have launched, and many have stalled on getting users to stick around and pay. Your own pain is real, but that alone is how people end up in this trap.
- 🔴 **Hypotheticals over past behaviour.** We don't know what any of the 10 people did the last time they needed to post, what they use now, or what they pay for it.
- 🟡 **Possibly network-only evidence.** If the 10 are mostly people who already know or follow you, this becomes a friends-only signal and the score caps at 25.
- 🟡 **No distribution plan.** No channel and no math. The default in this category is content plus ads against much bigger competitors.

**6 red flags → kill or pivot.**

## Pivot options

The core insight worth keeping: **the hard part is turning messy thoughts into writing that sounds like you, not pressing "publish."** Build on that half.

1. **One customer, one channel, one job tied to money.** For example: *solo B2B consultants or coaches who win clients through LinkedIn* turn a 3-minute voice note into a post that sounds like them. The pain is tied to revenue ("no posts = no inbound leads"), the segment is reachable, members refer each other, and switching from "post nothing" is easy. Leave multi-platform publishing out of version 1.
2. **Done-with-you service first, software second.** Founders already pay human ghostwriters hundreds to thousands per month. Sell a hands-on "send me your ideas, I return a week of posts in your voice, published" service at a founding price. Use AI behind the scenes and only automate what you repeat. This tests willingness to pay this week, without building anything.
3. **Different buyer: small agencies or freelancers handling content for several clients.** They have budget, the pain grows with every client (many voices, many approvals, many accounts), and one sale is worth several solo founders. Check whether "client approval + voice per client" is where they're bleeding time.

## The riskiest assumption

**Belief:** "A specific group of small-business founders will *pay* to turn rough ideas into publish-ready posts, rather than keep using a free AI chat tool plus a free or native scheduler."

**Falsification test:** Pick one narrow segment (pivot 1 is the suggested default). Offer your ~10 waitlist contacts, plus 20 strangers in that segment you reach directly, a paid **founding offer**: "Send me your raw ideas or a voice note each week; you get 5 posts in your voice, ready or published." Charge a real price, paid upfront for the first month (e.g. $29–49). Deliver it by hand with AI behind the scenes. Don't build the multi-platform publisher yet.

**Success/failure threshold:**
- **Pass:** ≥3 of 30 people pay upfront, *and* ≥2 of them are strangers outside your network.
- **Fail:** fewer than 2 people pay. Treat that as a kill for this segment, not a reason to build more features.
- Track whether payers still use it in week 3. Paying once and then going quiet is a weak pass.

**Deadline:** 2026-12-01 (14 days).

**Estimated cost:** ~$0–50 (a simple payment link and landing page) plus ~10–15 hours of outreach and hand delivery.
