# Brand Brief

## 1. Brand Snapshot

### Product Name
Levercast

### One-Sentence Description
Levercast turns your rough, spoken ideas into LinkedIn and X posts that sound like you — not like AI.

### Category
Reframed category: a voice-first thinking partner for consultants who should be posting but aren't. Adjacent to (but deliberately not positioned as) "AI writing assistants for LinkedIn" and social schedulers.

### Core Promise
Your thoughts, out of your head and into the world — in your voice, consistently, without the blank-page freeze.

### Strategic Summary
Solo B2B consultants win clients by being visible and showing how they think, yet many post nothing. Not because they lack ideas, but because the pressure to write the perfect post freezes them. The obvious fix, ChatGPT, makes it worse: the output sounds generic, salesy, and "AI," which is exactly what they fear being caught doing. Levercast wins by starting from a voice note (talking is easier than writing), learning the user's own writing style, refusing generic hooks, and evolving with them. The brand must therefore feel calm, human, and quietly confident. It celebrates the user's thinking and keeps the AI in the background.

## 2. Positioning

### Target Market
Solo B2B consultants (any specialism) whose client pipeline depends on being visible on LinkedIn and/or X, who are price-sensitive individuals paying out of their own pocket, and who currently post rarely or never.

Excludes: agencies managing multiple clients, consumer creators and influencers chasing reach, B2C small businesses, and teams with a marketing function.

### Primary Audience
The non-posting expert: a solo consultant with real expertise and a backlog of ideas who knows they should post but hasn't.

### Buyer And User
Same person. The consultant discovers, buys, and uses Levercast themselves. No approval chain.

### Problem
They have plenty of good ideas for posts, but the pressure to write a perfect post leaves them paralysed, so they post nothing. When they do try AI tools, the result sounds generic and salesy, and they're afraid peers and prospects will spot it as AI.

### Alternatives
- **Posting nothing (the real competitor):** the default state, driven by perfectionism.
- **ChatGPT or other general AI chat tools:** fast, but the output is generic, hook-heavy, and recognisably "AI."
- **Schedulers with AI writers (Buffer, Typefully, Taplio, etc.):** solve distribution, not the thinking-to-writing freeze.
- **Human ghostwriters:** they can sound like you, but they're expensive and need briefing.

### Differentiation
- **Starts from a voice note.** Talking is freer than writing, which removes the blank page.
- **Learns your writing style** and evolves as you use it, so drafts converge on how you actually sound.
- **No generic hooks.** It refuses engagement-bait openers and template structures.
- **Nothing auto-publishes.** You stay the author.

### Positioning Statement
For solo B2B consultants who know they should be posting on LinkedIn and X but freeze trying to write the perfect post, Levercast is a voice-first thinking partner that turns rough spoken ideas into posts that sound like them. Unlike ChatGPT, which produces generic AI slop, it learns their writing style, refuses generic hooks, and gets more like them over time.

### Category Expectations
- Draft quality that clearly beats pasting into ChatGPT.
- Support for LinkedIn and X formats and norms.
- Fast enough to fit between client calls.
- Clear control over what gets published.
- Affordable for an individual (low-cost tier).

### Beliefs To Challenge
- "I need to write a perfect post or none at all." → A clear, honest thought beats a polished nothing.
- "AI writing always sounds like AI." → It does when it starts from a prompt. It doesn't when it starts from *you*.
- "Posting is a writing skill I don't have." → It's a thinking skill you already have. You just need to say it out loud.
- "Being visible means being salesy." → Sharing how you think is the opposite of selling.

## 3. Audience And Persona

### Primary Persona
- **Name:** The Frozen Expert
- **Role:** Solo B2B consultant (any specialism), working alone, wins work through reputation and referrals and knows they need visibility on LinkedIn and X.
- **Context:** Mostly at their desk between client work. Has ideas during calls, walks, and project work, and notes them in scattered places. They never become posts. Sees peers posting consistently and feels behind.
- **Skill level:** Novice poster. Rarely posts and is unsure what works. Fluent in their own domain's vocabulary, but not in social-media jargon (hooks, engagement rate, algorithm).
- **Motivation:** Be seen as a credible, distinctive thinker in their field; post consistently; attract client interest without "doing marketing."
- **Anxiety:** Looking salesy. Being found out for using AI. Posting something generic that damages their reputation.
- **Objections:** Price ("another subscription for something I might not use"). Secondary: trust that the output won't sound like AI.
- **Success moment:** Reading the first draft and thinking, *"That sounds like me."*
- **Trust requirements:** Testimonials from consultants like them, a visible voice-note-to-post example, and certainty that nothing goes out without approval.
- **Buying trigger:** Curiosity, after seeing another consultant's post that sounded good, or a mention of Levercast.
- **Device:** Desktop-first.
- **Emotional state on arrival:** Skeptical, especially of AI.

### Secondary Personas
- None for now. Revisit if VAs or assistants start operating accounts on a consultant's behalf.

### Jobs To Be Done
- **Functional:** Get the ideas in my head into publish-ready LinkedIn and X posts quickly, in my voice.
- **Emotional:** Feel unblocked and relieved, not judged. Feel proud of what I post.
- **Social:** Be seen as a sharp, original thinker in my field, not as someone pushing AI-written content or selling.

### Awareness Level
**Problem-aware.** They know they should post and that they don't, but they haven't actively sought a solution (or have dismissed AI tools as slop). Implications: lead with the problem in their words ("You have the ideas. You just never post them."), name the paralysis, then show the mechanism. Don't lead with features or comparisons to competitors they haven't researched.

## 4. Brand Personality

### Personality Traits
- Honest, not harsh
- Encouraging, not cheerleading
- Sharp, not salesy
- Human, not "AI-powered"
- Calm, not passive
- Confident, not loud

### Voice Principles
- **Headlines:** Short, plain, slightly contrarian. Name the real problem (paralysis, slop) rather than promising growth. One idea per headline.
- **Body copy:** Conversational, second person, peer-to-peer. Short sentences. Sounds like a thoughtful colleague talking, not a marketer.
- **CTAs:** Low-pressure and action-literal ("Record your first idea," "Hear it back as a post"). Never urgency or hype.
- **Errors and empty states:** Gentle and specific. Take responsibility for failures, never blame the user, and never guilt-trip about not posting. Empty states give a nudge, not a lecture.
- **Sales and trust moments:** Show, don't claim. Put a real voice note next to the post it became, use testimonials in the consultant's own words, and state plainly that nothing publishes without approval. Frame price against the cost of staying invisible, not against other tools.
- **Humor:** Dry wit, used sparingly. Usually a knowing nod at AI slop or perfectionism. Never jokes at the user's expense.

### Words To Use
- your thoughts / your thinking
- your voice / sounds like you
- rough idea
- say it, then post it
- out of your head
- not slop
- draft (the user is always the author)
- consistent / show up
- your take / your point of view

### Words To Avoid
- AI-powered, AI-generated (in user-facing output especially)
- supercharge, 10x, unlock, game-changer
- viral, hacks, growth hacks
- content machine, content engine, pump out
- effortless (implies no thinking, which undermines the user's authorship)
- algorithm-beating, engagement bait, hooks (as a selling point)
- creator, influencer (wrong identity for consultants)
- ghostwriter (implies someone else is the author)

### Tone By Context
- **First impression:** Empathetic and contrarian. "Finally, someone who gets why I don't post."
- **Onboarding:** Calm and reassuring. One step at a time, low stakes ("Just talk. It doesn't need to be good.").
- **Core workflow:** Quiet and focused. Gets out of the way, with minimal chatter.
- **Error or failure:** Plain, responsible, brief. Say what happened and what to do next.
- **Success moment:** Understated recognition ("That's you."). No confetti language.
- **Sales or conversion moment:** Honest and grounded. Show the evidence, name the price, frame it against the cost of not posting.

## 5. Messaging System

### Primary Message
You have the ideas. Levercast turns them into posts that sound like you, not like AI.

### Supporting Messages
- **Just talk.** Start from a voice note. No blank page, no perfect first sentence.
- **Sounds like you.** It learns your writing style and gets closer to you every time.
- **No slop, no hooks.** No generic openers, no engagement bait, nothing that reads as AI.
- **You're always the author.** Nothing is published until you approve it.

### Narrative Arc
- **Before:** A head full of good ideas, a feed full of other people's posts, and nothing of yours on it.
- **Struggle:** Writing the perfect post feels impossible, and AI tools make you sound like everyone else, or worse, like a bot.
- **Breakthrough:** You don't need to write. You need to say what you think. Levercast turns your spoken thinking into your written voice.
- **After:** You post consistently, people recognise your point of view, and conversations with prospects start from your posts.

### Proof Points
- Side-by-side demo: a raw voice note next to the finished post.
- Testimonials from solo consultants in their own words, especially "it sounds like me."
- Transparent control: an explicit approve-before-publish step.
- Mechanism explanation: how style learning works, and what "no generic hooks" means in practice.
- (Later) Before-and-after posting consistency from real users.

### Objection Handling
- **Objection:** "It's another subscription. I'm not sure it's worth paying for."
  **Response:** Compare it to the cost of staying invisible: one client conversation from a post pays for a year. Use low-cost pricing and an easy first-use experience so they reach "that sounds like me" before paying much.
- **Objection:** "It'll sound like AI and people will know."
  **Response:** It starts from your words and your voice note, learns your style, and refuses generic hooks. Show a side-by-side example. You approve everything.
- **Objection:** "I'll look salesy."
  **Response:** Levercast helps you share how you think, not pitch. No templates and no engagement bait.
- **Objection:** "I don't have time to post."
  **Response:** If you can talk for two minutes, you can post.

### Claims And Evidence
- **Claim:** "Posts that sound like you."
  **Evidence needed:** User testimonials confirming it, plus a demo comparison. Usable at launch with real examples.
- **Claim:** "No generic hooks / no AI slop."
  **Evidence needed:** The product must enforce it (style rules, hook filtering), with demonstrable examples.
- **Claim:** "Gets more like you over time."
  **Evidence needed:** A working style-learning feature, plus a user-visible indication of evolution.
- **Claim:** "More engagement" / "more client interest."
  **Evidence needed:** Testimonials or data from real users. **Do not use until evidence exists.**
- **Claim:** "Post consistently."
  **Evidence needed:** Usage data showing repeat weekly posting. Use softly ("helps you show up") until then.

## 6. Brand Experience Principles

### Experience Promises
- Starting is easy: a voice note is enough, and no writing is required to begin.
- The first draft sounds recognisably like the user.
- The user is always the author. Nothing is published without explicit approval.
- It gets better the more you use it.

### Product Behavior Principles
- Never pressure the user to post. Invite, don't nag.
- Explain itself plainly: what it changed from the voice note and why, when useful.
- Handle uncertainty by asking, not guessing. If an idea is thin, ask a clarifying question rather than padding with generic filler.
- Preserve the user's phrasing and opinions. Edit for clarity, not to sound smarter.
- Keep the AI invisible in outputs. No tell-tale AI phrasing, no "As an expert…" openers, no emoji-bullet templates.

### Trust And Credibility
- Trust signal: testimonials from solo consultants describing the "sounds like me" moment.
- Risk reducer: approve-before-publish, always. Easy to edit, easy to discard.
- Proof requirement: a visible voice-note-to-post example before signup.

### Things The Brand Must Never Do
- Never auto-publish or post on the user's behalf without approval.
- Never use engagement-bait templates, generic hooks, or "viral" formulas.
- Never brand the user's output as AI-generated, or market the product as "AI-powered" first.
- Never guilt-trip users for not posting.
- Never promise engagement or client results without evidence.
- Never make the user sound salesy.

## 7. Market And Competitive Context

### Alternatives
- Posting nothing (status quo, the main competitor)
- ChatGPT, Claude, and other general AI chat tools
- Schedulers with built-in AI writing (Buffer, Typefully, Taplio, Hootsuite, etc.)
- LinkedIn-specific AI post generators
- Human ghostwriters

### Differentiation Map
- **We are more:** personal, voice-first, calm, focused on the writing job, in the user's own style.
- **We are less:** feature-heavy, growth-hacky, analytics-obsessed, multi-platform-first.
- **We are similar to:** a thoughtful editor or writing partner who knows how you talk.
- **We are different from:** schedulers, content-repurposing machines, and template-driven AI post generators.

### Category Language
- **Terms to use:** voice note, draft, your voice, your take, post, thinking partner.
- **Terms to avoid:** content engine, AI copywriter, ghostwriter, scheduler (as the lead identity), creator tools.
- **Concepts that need explanation:** how style learning works; why starting from voice produces less generic writing; what "no generic hooks" means.

## 8. Content And Naming Rules

### Naming Conventions
- **Product features:** Plain, literal names describing what the user does or gets (e.g. "Voice note," "Your style," "Drafts"). No coined jargon or "AI" prefixes.
- **Plans or tiers:** Simple and honest (e.g. "Free," "Solo"). Avoid "Pro," "Growth," and "Ultimate" style escalation.
- **Actions and CTAs:** Verb-first, literal, user-as-author ("Record an idea," "Review draft," "Post to LinkedIn").
- **Events or states:** Calm, descriptive language ("Draft ready," "Posted," "Needs your review"). No celebration overload.

### CTA Patterns
- **Primary CTA:** Verb plus the user's own thing, low pressure: "Record your first idea," "Turn an idea into a post."
- **Secondary CTA:** Show proof: "Hear a voice note become a post," "See an example."
- **Avoid:** Urgency and hype ("Start growing now," "Go viral," "Supercharge your content"), and anything that implies the AI writes for you.

### Microcopy Rules
- Write in second person, conversational, short sentences.
- Refer to output as "your draft" or "your post," never "AI content."
- Empty states nudge gently ("What's been on your mind this week?"). Never shame ("You haven't posted in 14 days!").
- Errors: say what happened, own it, give the next step. No jokes in errors.
- Keep success messages understated ("Posted. Nice one.").
